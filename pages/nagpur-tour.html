Overview

Nagpur, popularly known as the Orange City, is the winter capital of the state of Maharashtra. Located at the geographical center of India, the city holds historical and cultural significance. It is the largest city in central India and the third largest city in Maharashtra.

The city is famous for its oranges, which are a major agricultural product of the region. Nagpur is also known as the Tiger Capital of India as it connects many tiger reserves in the country. The city serves as the headquarters of the RSS and houses the historic Deekshabhoomi, where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar embraced Buddhism.

With its strategic location, pleasant climate, and rich heritage, Nagpur has emerged as a major commercial and political center in central India. The Zero Mile Stone, which marks the geographical center of colonial India, is located here.

Tourist Attractions

Discover the iconic landmarks and cultural hotspots that make Nagpur a must-visit destination

Deekshabhoomi

A sacred monument of Buddhism and the largest hollow stupa in the world. This is where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar converted to Buddhism along with 500,000 followers on October 14, 1956.

Central Nagpur

Zero Mile Stone

A monument built by the British during the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India. It marks the geographical center of colonial India and consists of a pillar and four stucco horses.

Civil Lines

Dragon Palace Temple

Also known as Lotus Temple of Nagpur, this Buddhist temple features beautiful architecture and a serene meditation hall. The temple houses a large statue of Buddha and is surrounded by beautifully landscaped gardens.

Kamptee Road

Orange Orchards

Experience the famous Nagpur oranges at their source. The region produces some of India's finest citrus fruits. Visit during winter season from November to February to see the orchards in full bloom and taste fresh oranges.
